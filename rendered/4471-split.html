The problems

parser · the shared parser reads every date month first. A tenant whose locale writes the day first (every tenant on file) gets its dates flipped: 7 April comes out as 4 July. This is the bug #4471 names, and the queued fix is for it. It is by far the largest group.

hint · booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under the date field. On the day-first tenants that run booking-v3, people who book through the form type what the hint tells them, month first, and the form reads it the tenant’s way. The parser fix does not touch this: the dates are wrong when they are typed. Only form reports can be this problem; the same tenants’ app and email reports are parser.

Reports read as a one-day move. Some reports describe a date a day off, not flipped: a Tuesday invite for a Monday booking, an invite sent before the booking existed, one meeting that reached Lisbon and Toronto on different days. The desk keeps them where they are filed, under parser, and footnotes them (desk-notes.json). They are counted in the parser row above, not as a problem of their own. Engineering should read them when the parser fix lands: if the day is still off, time zones are the next place to look.

What went wrong in the merge

  • Intake tags come from the reporter’s browser, a guess made at the door. They disagree with the cause from the tenant’s data, so a tag is a hint to read the report, not a reason to merge.
  • The export repeats some rows. Count by id, once.
  • A few reports carry no dates at all. They stay under the cause their tenant gives, and a human should read them when the fix lands.

Lessons

  • Merge on cause, not on subject. The cause comes from the tenant and the channel (triage still says otherwise, and decision 0001 is accepted: a new record has to replace it, nobody edits it).
  • A table that adds up to the ticket is not proof that the ticket is one problem.
  • Numbers on this page are computed from the export when the page is built. If the desk’s data changes, the page changes with it.
